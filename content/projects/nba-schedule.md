I started playing fantasy basketball in the 13-14 season and I was not very good. The next
season I developed my first data-driven approach to fantasy sports. I noticed that there were
certain days of the week with more games on them (wednesday, friday) and certain days with
fewer games (tuesday, thursday). This is a quirk of the national TV schedule, which means that
the teams that tend to play more on those days are not random. By identifying which teams were
more likely to play on the same nights as other teams, you'd be able to reduce how many times
per season your roster had an excess of players who couldn't play. Halfway through that
season, I looked at the schedules of a few teams and made a determination to trade players who
I thought were over-valued because of their schedule for players that were under-valued for
their schedule. I ended up missing the finals because of a single Paul George heat check in
his return from injury game.

I have a rule with fantasy sports. If I don't think the dumbest person in my league is capable
of coming up with and executing an analysis, I share it with everyone because otherwise it
feels like cheating. While anyone could do what I had done the season before, I spent the
summer trying to turn it into a usable metric. I used normalization to come up with a score I
thought was useful. Although it's hard to judge the actual tangible success of something like
this, we did have a team draft according to that strategy, never set their lineup (so their
starters were by default the 9 best players on the team), and proceed to make the playoffs. To
me, that shows there was some value.

A couple years later I added a matrix that compares teams to each other. The idea behind this
is that your depth players shouldn't conflict with your headliners. If I draft a player in the
first few rounds, I don't want the other players on my team at that position to all play the
same schedule.

The efficacy of this strategy obviously needs more rigorous testing, but I think it's a
reasonable way to think about the problem of roster optimization post draft.
